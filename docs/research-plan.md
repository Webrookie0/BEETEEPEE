# SwarmFlow: follow, yield, recover

Research and implementation plan, 26 September 2026. Independent project; no source copied from the existing `btp` project or QuadSwarm.

## Problem definition

A cooperative swarm follows a prescribed reference target at a stand-off distance. Every drone owns a persistent formation slot. An obstacle can cross the flight path; drones temporarily leave their slots, avoid the obstacle and one another, then recover their original slots relative to the moving target. “Return” means the same target-relative slot, not an obsolete world coordinate. No interception, capture, pursuit-evasion, or contact objective is included. The target trajectory is exogenous and does not respond to the swarm.

The research question is: **Can a decentralized learned controller reduce formation disturbance and recovery time while matching the collision rate of a predictive classical controller, including on unseen obstacle trajectories and swarm sizes?** This is a hypothesis to test, not an established novelty claim.

## What the literature actually supports

1. **Batra et al., Decentralized Control of Quadrotor Swarms with End-to-end Deep Reinforcement Learning, CoRL 2021.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07735), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.07735v2). Sections 3–4 and the supplement were read. Uses shared policies, local neighbor position/velocity, deep sets/attention, PPO, direct thrust, and randomized formations. Section 4.4 explicitly includes moving spherical obstacles and formation re-establishment. Therefore our basic behavior already has precedent. Borrow the relative observation structure, permutation invariance and separate collision/formation measurements. Their substantial training budget (one billion samples per policy in the reported setup) is not a promise of laptop training speed.
2. **Huang et al., Collision Avoidance and Navigation for a Quadrotor Swarm Using End-to-end Deep Reinforcement Learning, ICRA 2024.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13285), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.13285v2). Method and ablations were read. Introduces signed-distance obstacle observations, attention, and resets from states before collisions. Its main dense-obstacle formulation uses static full-height obstacles; that is different from our moving-object benchmark. The replay mechanism resets environments to saved states and collects fresh trajectories: it is not permission to train on stale PPO transitions. Their nearest-obstacle ablation performs poorly in dense gaps. Our small spherical-object observation is therefore an initial scope limit; dense walls/corridors require distance/ray observations before making general navigation claims.
3. **Huang et al., QuadSwarm: A Modular Multi-Quadrotor Simulator for Deep Reinforcement Learning with Direct Thrust Control, ICRA 2023 workshop.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09537), [repository](https://github.com/Zhehui-Huang/quad-swarm-rl/). Abstract and repository README reviewed. Separating simulation from rendering, scenario generation and randomization are useful design principles. This is a workshop simulator paper, not an independent guarantee of robustness. The repository is a reference, not our base implementation.
4. **Yu et al., The Surprising Effectiveness of PPO in Cooperative, Multi-Agent Games, NeurIPS 2022 Datasets and Benchmarks.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01955). Abstract and publication metadata reviewed. Supports using cooperative PPO as a serious baseline, but does not establish drone performance. We start with parameter-sharing independent PPO and a local critic. A centralized critic is a subsequent controlled comparison; calling the initial implementation MAPPO would be inaccurate.
5. **Panerati et al., Learning to Fly — a Gym Environment with PyBullet Physics for Reinforcement Learning of Multi-agent Quadcopter Control, IROS 2021.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02142). Abstract and publication metadata reviewed. Provides a path to full multirotor physics, aerodynamic effects and controller comparisons. Use this as a candidate validation backend after the behavior benchmark is stable.
6. **van den Berg et al., Reciprocal n-body Collision Avoidance, ISRR proceedings, 2011.** [Official ORCA project and publication](https://gamma-web.iacs.umd.edu/ORCA/). Official formulation and publication listing reviewed. Predicting relative motion matters; reciprocal agents split avoidance responsibility. Our sampled predictive baseline is **not ORCA**, does not implement its linear program, and inherits none of its formal guarantees. Moving external objects must be treated as non-cooperative.

## Direction and alternatives

Start with **RL for swarm movement over stabilized flight**, rather than direct motor RL. Motor learning combines stabilization, coordination and avoidance in one difficult optimization problem. It makes failures hard to diagnose and is not necessary to test this project's main question. Retain direct motor control as a later research branch only if aggressive dynamics become central.

Use persistent virtual slots rather than pure flocking. Separation/alignment/cohesion alone cannot require drone 3 to return to drone 3's prior place. Keep slot identities constant during an episode, including during avoidance. Use a fixed world-aligned formation frame initially so target reversals do not instantaneously rotate the slots into one another. Heading-aligned formations need a rate-limited frame and are a later comparison.

Use a fast NumPy stabilized-flight model for iteration. It integrates 3D position and velocity with limited acceleration and finite controller response. It is **not** a six-degree-of-freedom rotor simulator; attitude in the viewer is illustrative. No downwash, contact response, camera perception, motor torque or sim-to-real claim is included. Detect collisions continuously over each integration segment and fail the episode; never teleport drones away from overlap. Later replay the same scenarios in PyBullet/gym-pybullet-drones before interpreting them as physically validated drone maneuvers.

Keep the classical policy visible. The default interactive demonstration uses a sampled predictive controller. Learned evaluation uses only the checkpoint's network, without a hidden collision-avoidance controller. Optionally pretrain the actor using classical demonstrations, then use fresh on-policy PPO rollouts. Report imitation and RL distinctly.

## Environment contract

- PettingZoo parallel API, one agent per drone, simultaneous actions. Gymnasium spaces define the tensors. Shared policy weights permit different swarm sizes.
- Actions: three normalized desired velocity components, with a vector speed limit, passed to a finite-response acceleration-limited flight model at 20 Hz with physics substeps.
- Self observation: slot error, velocity, target velocity, previous command, slot offset, altitude and time remaining. Neighbor and obstacle observations: relative position/velocity, radius, visibility mask. A radius cap and nearest-object capacity prevent access to every object's state. Sensor noise/dropout are optional.
- The actor receives no future path, obstacle event schedule, global swarm centroid, or other agents' goals. Each local observation includes a shared target reference and its own assigned slot. Perfect target broadcast is an explicit first-stage assumption.
- Masked set encoders handle neighbor ordering. The critic initially has the same information as the actor (IPPO). Centralized information belongs only in a future critic experiment.
- Dense per-second costs: slot error, velocity mismatch, proximity, command effort and command changes. Collision/world-boundary costs are terminal events. Avoid a large alive reward that makes stopping profitable or negative living costs that make crashing profitable; use a substantial failure cost and inspect failure behavior empirically.
- Same seeded scenarios and physics for classical and learned comparisons. Failure terminates all agents. Time limits truncate and bootstrap critic values from the final observation, without allowing GAE to cross a reset.

## Scenarios and measurements

Initial suite: stationary formation, moving-target follow, lateral crossing object, head-on object, two crossing objects, static spheres on the path, a smoothly turning target, and a wind gust. Viewer injection supports additional crossing objects with configurable speed and radius; insertion does not guarantee a feasible escape. Any collision counts as a failure.

Track episode collision/failure rate, minimum surface clearance, time-weighted RMS slot error, target-relative centroid error, command smoothness, episode duration, and recovery time for each injected event. A crossing event has a scheduled end when the object has traversed the formation region. Recovery is achieved when **every drone** is within 0.35 m of its own slot and within 0.35 m/s of target velocity continuously for one second after that end. Missed windows remain unrecovered; multiple events are tracked separately. Report recovery rate and conditional time together, since dropping failures would bias the result.

Proposed acceptance targets, not literature-derived guarantees: no collisions over 100 held-out nominal episodes per scenario; at least 95% event recovery within 5 s; steady-state RMS slot error below 0.35 m. Zero failures in 100 trials still permits a roughly 3% upper failure probability (rule of three); report uncertainty and the complete seed list. Stress tests should identify failure boundaries, not silently exclude difficult episodes.

## Ordered execution plan

1. **Runnable benchmark:** seeded environment, immutable slot identity, finite-response dynamics, swept collisions, classical controller, browser viewer, interactive injections, JSON reports, API tests and baseline scenario regression. Completed implementation and evidence are described in README and `docs/validation.md`.
2. **Learning infrastructure:** masked set actor/critic, optional behavior cloning, clipped PPO, correct time-limit handling, checkpoints with environment configuration, and held-out evaluation. Smoke-test training and checkpoint reload. A smoke checkpoint is not a successful learned controller.
3. **Training study:** run at least 3 independent training seeds; stages hold → follow → crossing/head-on → mixed disturbances. Advance on validation metrics, not merely elapsed training. Retain earlier tasks in each stage. Estimate throughput on the actual machine before committing compute. Separate validation seeds from an untouched final test set. Do not tune on final tests.
4. **Ablations:** cloning alone vs PPO from scratch vs cloning+PPO; local vs centralized critic; current-distance vs velocity-aware observations; fixed vs adaptive formation penalty; sensor noise/dropout; neighbor capacity; original slots vs free reassignment. Keep environment transitions and optimizer budgets matched. Failure-state resets can be added with fresh rollouts, as in Huang 2024.
5. **Generalization:** train initially with 8 agents; evaluate 4/8/12/16, faster/larger/oblique objects, repeated crossings, wind and imperfect sensing. Densities and difficulty must be stated; passing one easy crossing is insufficient.
6. **Physics validation:** choose a supported PyBullet drone backend, calibrate stable velocity control, measure tracking lag, add thrust/tilt limits and downwash-aware separation, re-run held-out trajectories, then fine-tune with physical parameter randomization. Dense static corridors require distance/ray sensing and an appropriate planner comparison. Hardware flight is a separate future project.

The immediate deliverable is stages 1–2 plus measured baseline evidence. Training convergence, publication-level comparisons, dense-obstacle navigation and physics/hardware validation remain explicit experiments, not features to claim from a short run.
